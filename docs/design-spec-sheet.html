01 / read before anything elseThree rules that override every other page here

These are not preferences and they are not negotiable at the design stage. Work that breaks one of them gets sent back regardless of how good it is.

Locked The logo is used as-is. Never redrawn.

Do not redraw, recolour, distort or re-typeset the lockup. Do not extract a piece of it. Do not set the wordmark in a lookalike font. The "Market" lockup is the master mark everywhere — header, hero, footer — and Location pages set their own name in type beside it rather than getting their own mark.

One carve-out, approved 27 Aug 2026: the paddle alone is the icon mark, for the small square slots the lockup physically cannot fill — favicon, app icon, avatar. There it ships flat white on a navy disc, which is a recolour, and the 16px size carries a deliberately thickened paddle. That approval covers the icon and nothing else. A bare paddle never stands in for the lockup in the header, the hero, or any other lockup slot.

Cultural guardrail Do not draw Coast Salish formline.

Coast Salish formline is specific to Lummi Nation, and imitations — hand-drawn, stock or AI-generated — can be inaccurate or appropriative. Every motif currently in the build is a flat placeholder silhouette that looks like a placeholder on purpose: a convincing imitation is the failure mode, not the goal.

Final art must be authentic, commissioned, or tribe-approved before launch. If you are supplying interim art, keep it to simple flat silhouettes from the approved subject list — canoe, paddle, eagle, salmon, orca, crab, moving water — and mark every file TODO: replace with approved Lummi art.

Scope Other Lummi companies appear exactly once.

The wider corporate group is represented by a single footer link reading "Lummi Commercial Companies" and nowhere else — not in artwork, not in copy, not in navigation. Do not introduce a second logo, a parent-company endorsement line, or a corporate lockup anywhere in an asset.

Two blues that are inside the artwork and are not UI colours

#000F9F (0, 15, 159) is the original brand blue and lives inside the logo file only. #1D3D7C (29, 61, 124) is the navy actually present in the supplied raster art. Neither is a UI colour and neither may be sampled out of the logo and used on a surface — the interface navy is #1C4E8F. Picking a colour out of the logo with an eyedropper is the most common way this rule gets broken.

Contrast, as a working rule

Body text must reach WCAG AA, 4.5:1. Navy, deep-navy and ink all pass on ground and paper. Teal and cedar do not — they are large-graphic colours. If an accent has to carry small text, darken it first and measure it; do not assume. Where an element crosses both light and dark grounds, this project's established fix is a bone #F5F1E8 rim, which is why the tab icons carry one: without it the navy disc measured 1.19:1 on Chrome's dark chrome and simply dissolved.

Social icons are drawn here, not fetched from the platform

Each social icon is a single inline SVG held in this repository, filled with --lb-bone — never the platform's own colour, never its official badge or follow button, and never a file served from the platform's servers. The reason is not aesthetic. An official widget loads third-party code and sets a cookie before anyone clicks it, and the site's privacy policy states in plain words that this site sets no cookies. So the icon set is a drawing job: Facebook, Instagram and Yelp, in that order, Yelp last because customers speak there and the business does not.

Location cards carry a 26 × 26 motif, not a photograph — do not supply store photography for them. The fuel-price block carries no art at all: it is the most-used thing on the site and it is a table.

Motifs ship as masks, not as coloured artwork

Supply decorative motifs as solid shapes on a transparent background, in SVG. The site uses them as a mask-image over a brand colour, so the shape comes from your file and the colour comes from the token layer — your file's colours are ignored. That is deliberate: a motif then cannot introduce an off-brand colour, and re-tinting every motif on the site is one value.

Header motifs: what the upload box accepts (30 Sep 2026)

Staff upload header motifs themselves, into a motif library of their own, and arrange them into groups — an owl, a whale and a fish as three files in order, or one file holding all three. The band draws them in one row at the group's scale, drops whole motifs that do not fit, and never cuts one in half. Every file is checked before it is uploaded, and the box says in plain words why one is refused:

RefusedWhy

See-through parts are allowed but show fainter than the rest; the band's overall strength (0–40%, 14% approved) is set per group in the CMS, not in the file. Uploading a motif does not approve it: Coast Salish art must be authentic or tribe-approved before launch.

This is the one asset on this sheet that is not supplied at 2×

Everywhere else, export at twice the CSS size. Not here: 1200 × 630 is the delivered pixel size, platforms re-encode to their own dimensions, and a 2400 × 1260 file buys nothing but weight. The floor matters more than the ceiling — below 600 × 315 the large card silently degrades to a small square thumbnail beside the text, with no warning and no error.

Nothing that can go out of date may be drawn on this card

No fuel prices, no price of any kind, no phone number, no hours, no address. Platforms cache an unfurl against the page URL for days to indefinitely, so a card outlives the fact printed on it — and a link already shared keeps the old image even after the file is replaced. A new card ships as a new filename, never an overwrite.

The page title and description are live text supplied by the platform beside the image, so do not set the page title inside the artwork — it appears twice. A short standing line is fine; unlike a promo, nothing is overlaid on this image.

No tribal motif on a share card until the approved art exists

Interim motifs elsewhere get swapped before launch. A card that has been scraped cannot be recalled — it sits in other companies' caches. The waterline is the single exception, being an abstract gradient wave band rather than formline.

The card is a lockup slot, so the paddle icon carve-out does not apply: a bare paddle never stands in for the lockup here, whatever the crop does.

All five crops were signed off on 16 September 2026 and recorded in ADR 0023 — build to them. What was already locked is the column ladder and a 150px minimum slot height; the crops clear that floor and hold every row height inside a consistent band (158–258px on the built site). The slot widths are on-screen sizes, not file sizes. A sharp (retina) screen draws two real pixels for every on-screen one, so the 1168 × 234 full-width slot needs 2336 × 468 real pixels — which is why the file is 2400 wide. And because every slot is a horizontal strip across the whole width of the file, differing only in how many rows it keeps, the file has to be as tall as the tallest strip: the 16:9 quarter and phone slots keep all 1350 rows, while the full-width slot keeps only the middle 480. So one 2400 × 1350 file holds every slot at 2× — the 2× column shows that, it is not a list of files to make. The tablet crop (5:2) was added when the site was built, so a tablet held upright does not get a 16:9 slot 480px tall; it sits between the ladder's own ratios and cuts only top and bottom, like every other crop. In a mixed row (wide + narrow, or lead + two) the slots keep their own ratios, as the approved ladder draws them, so the wide one is taller than the narrow one beside it. A row re-divides when a promo expires, so the art gets re-cropped

Twelve divides evenly by 1, 2, 3, 4 and 6. That is the point: a three-across row holding two live promos becomes two halves, and holding one it becomes full width — a hole can never appear. The consequence for you is that a promo you designed as a quarter may be displayed as a half or full width, without anyone re-supplying art.

So keep the subject centred, away from the edges, and never let the composition depend on a specific crop. On a phone every row collapses to one column regardless of its desktop layout.

Supply one master per promo — 2400 × 1350, subject inside the centre strip

16:9 is the tallest crop on the ladder and 5:1 the widest, so a 16:9 master is only ever cut from the top and bottom — never horizontally. Supply 2400 × 1350 and keep the subject inside the centre 2400 × 480 band, and the same file serves all five desktop slots, the tablet, the phone, and the hero of the info page the promo links to. One file per promo — not two, and not one per ratio.

That centre band is where the full-width 5:1 crop lands; everything above and below it is breathing room the narrower slots use. Anything that has to be seen — a face, a product, a logo on a cup — belongs inside the band.

Never bake copy into a promo image

Eyebrow, headline and button are live text fields the store staff type, laid over your image. Text inside the artwork will be duplicated, will not translate, will not scale on a phone, and will not be readable by a screen reader. Supply imagery only — and leave the image dark enough, or quiet enough, in the area where that text sits.

How much text, signed off 16 September 2026: three fields — eyebrow, headline, button — on every slot and every device. There is no body field. The headline is capped at 28 characters, which is short enough that nothing wraps. The button label is editable too, and defaults to “See details”.

Where the text sits, as built (30 Sep 2026): in the bottom-left corner of every slot, on a navy shade drawn behind the words only. The shade is 92px tall (90 on a phone) and about 230–300px wide — as wide as the words, fading out above them and to their right. Everything else in the frame is left unshaded. So:

  • Keep faces, products and logos out of the bottom-left corner. On a full-width promo that corner is about a quarter of the width; the rest of the picture is clear.
  • In the narrowest slots the words span nearly the full width. In the 282 × 158 quarter the shade covers the bottom 58% of the frame, and on a phone about half of it — so the top of the frame is the part guaranteed clear. Compose the subject high in the centre band, or accept that small slots show it partly behind the words.
  • The site sets the shade's darkness, not you — it is at least 80% navy where any word sits, so the text stays readable over a white photograph. You do not need to darken the image for it.

Tribal motifs are not permitted in the promo region. It already competes for attention; decorating it makes the page shout twice.

Never name a promo file ad, advert, banner or sponsored

Those exact strings sit on every ad-blocker filter list and are matched against filenames, class names and ids. A first-party promo named that way is silently hidden from a large share of visitors, with nothing in the build to indicate anything is wrong. Name files for what they show — piroshky-winter-2026.jpg, not promo-banner-3.jpg.

09 / files & weightThere is a hard 100 MB ceiling on the whole library

The content system's free tier allows 100 MB of assets in total, across the entire site, forever. A real set of store photography exceeds that on its own — so file weight is a live constraint on what can be commissioned, not a technical footnote.

At an average of 250 KB, 100 MB is roughly 400 images for the life of the site. Strip EXIF, convert to sRGB, and do not upload camera originals. If the photography library is going to be large, that is a decision to raise early — the alternatives are hosting images outside the CMS or changing plan, and both are cheaper to choose than to retrofit.

Watch for bitmaps wearing an SVG costume

A file ending .svg is not necessarily vector. One supplied to this project turned out to be a single base64 PNG in an SVG wrapper, with zero <path> elements and none of the benefits. To check in five seconds, open it in a text editor: <path> or <polygon> means real vector; <image> with base64 means a bitmap. Also confirm the root element has a viewBox, and that text has been converted to outlines — live <text> reflows wherever the brand font is not installed.

11 / summaryDo and don't

Do

  • Make navy dominant. Teal and cedar are accents, and the waterline is the recurring signature that ties pages together.
  • Supply one 2400 × 1350 master for every promo, with the subject inside the centre 2400 × 480 band. It serves all five desktop slots, the tablet, the phone and the promo's info page.
  • Keep faces, products and logos out of the bottom-left corner of promo art — the words sit there.
  • Export at 2× the CSS size listed, in sRGB, with EXIF stripped.
  • Keep the subject centred in promo art, so it survives a row re-dividing.
  • Send motifs as white-on-transparent SVG masks.
  • Mark every placeholder motif TODO: replace with approved Lummi art.
  • Compose the share card for the centred square — 285px is cut off each side in a chat thumbnail.
  • Name files for what they show.

Don't

  • Touch the logo — no redraw, no recolour, no re-typeset, no extracting a piece of it.
  • Sample a colour out of the logo art. #000F9F and #1D3D7C are not UI colours.
  • Set body copy or small labels in teal or cedar. They fail AA on light grounds.
  • Bake headlines, prices or buttons into a promo image — a ready-made social graphic with its words in it is exactly this.
  • Supply a separate image for an info page. It uses the promo's.
  • Use --lb-navy-deep for decoration. It belongs to fuel prices.
  • Put artwork in the promo region, the fuel-price block, or behind body copy.
  • Draw Coast Salish formline, or pass interim art off as final.
  • Put a price, a phone number or opening hours on a share card. It is cached for weeks after the fact changes.
  • Add a typeface or a colour that is not on this sheet.

Specifications are drawn from the project's locked brand system and its recorded architecture decisions. A figure derived rather than approved carries a Proposed marker — treat those as a question to settle, not a number to build to; there are none left on this sheet.
