Same rule at both widths. Condensed the bar carries Truck Stop, so Exit 260 moves into the panel.

Always loading collapsed removed a whole branch

ADR 0005 carried two opening mechanisms and a rule to choose between them — a panel already open on arrival cannot overlay — because Home and /fuel-prices loaded expanded. Nothing loads expanded now, so the in-flow disclosure variant is gone and every block opens the same way: a popover over the page. One mechanism, one set of behaviours to test, and the rule that picked between them is no longer needed.

It also unblocks Home. Condensing needs the resting height reserved, and an expanded block was too tall to reserve; a collapsed one is not. Home condenses like every other page now, which is what was asked for and is simpler than the exception it replaces.

Promo widths: a ladder, not free percentages

Each promo picks from full · two-thirds · half · third · quarter on a 12-column grid, so any mix tiles exactly. The Home frame above runs half + quarter + quarter — measured 398 / 192 / 192 in an 810px region. Free-typed percentages were rejected: 45% + 45% leaves a 10% orphan, and staff editing a set months later have no way to know what the other promos are using.

A 20% promo is below the readable floor on most desktops. Measured: 20% of a 1280px viewport is 247px of content and 279px at 1440 — narrower than a 375px phone screen, for artwork meant to carry an offer. Only at 1920 does it reach 375px. A quarter is what 20% usually means in practice, and it holds up. On a phone the ladder is ignored entirely and every promo is full width.

Fixed: the condensed bar could not be expanded

My bug, and in the one state the bar exists for. Condensing set display: none on the panel to stop an already-open panel hanging off a bar mid-scroll — which also made “All prices” do nothing once condensed. The <details> element dutifully reported open: true with nothing on screen, so it looked broken rather than blocked.

The right split: condensing closes the panel (in the observer, one line) and never prevents it. The panel also has to hang off the bar, not off the block’s reserved box, or it opens level with the space the collapsed card left behind. Verified in all four frames — auto-closes on condense, reopens on click, 8px under the bar, inside the viewport, carrying only the places not on screen.

Reserving the resting height is now a rule at every width

It was a phone-only fix. Then the desktop interior page moved 10px when the block condensed, and the reason Home had not was luck — its hero happened to be the taller grid item, so the row height did not depend on the block. Now the block keeps its resting height everywhere: the collapsed face and the cue stay in the layout and go invisible, and the one-line bar is drawn over the space they reserve. Measured at 0px on all four frames.

Two structural traps in the pinned rail

The rail column has to span the whole page, not the top region — given only the hero's height to stick within, the condensed bar vanished a screen later. And it must be pointer-events: none with the block itself re-enabled, or an empty column swallows clicks on the full-width sections beneath it.

The trap that cost the most time: grid-row: 1 / -1 silently collapsed to row 1. With no explicit grid-template-rows, -1 resolves to the end of the explicit grid — which is line 1 — so the page quietly lost half its height rather than erroring. 1 / span 2 is what was meant.

A page never advertises itself — including the Truck Stop

Decided: the callout drops on /truck-stop and stays on the Exit 260 page. So Exit 260 carries both the callout and its own truck-stop summary — deliberately, since that page is the truck stop’s home and two routes to it is not a duplication problem. The rule is now the same shape as the Location one: a page never advertises itself.

The location row now fills the column at 50/50

The cards were on a fixed three-track grid, so a Location page — which drops its own card — left 288px of dead space on the right. Switched to repeat(auto-fit, minmax(220px,1fr)): the vacated track collapses and the two survivors split the column, measured 398px each in 810px. Home still renders thirds at 261px. Moving the motif beside the name instead of above it took the desktop card from 151px to 108px tall, and VISIT is floor-aligned so the links line up when the descriptions differ in length.

Worth naming: the fixed grid looked right on Home and wrong on every Location page. Home is what gets reviewed, so that is the class of bug that ships.

One table, and the Truck Stop where it belongs

The block was two stacked tables with their own header rows, plus an ALSO AT caption on the panel — three separated blocks of numbers reading as three objects. Now it is one grid, one header row, a column per grade, with an em-dash where a place does not sell one. The panel is a continuation of the card’s table: no caption, no repeated headers, just a hairline between the rows already on the page and the rest.

The desktop condensed view was also dropping truck prices entirely. The rule was already right — the panel carries whatever is not on screen — but it had only ever been applied to Locations. The one-line bar carries a single Location, so the Truck Stop is off screen there and belongs in the panel. It now appears, DEF column and all, and disappears again at rest where the card shows it.

The phone card is now the desktop card. It carries the Location and the Truck Stop on arrival at both widths, and condenses to the Exit 260 line at the same point — half the card’s height. That deletes a per-width branch rather than adding one: truck prices are the reason a whole class of guests is on the site, and a phone is what they are holding.

The column set follows the rows that are on screen, not the rows that exist. DEF is a Truck Stop grade, so the DEF column lives wherever the Truck Stop is a row — the resting card at both widths, and the panel once the block condenses and the Truck Stop moves into it. Printing a grade column that no visible row sells is a column of em-dashes pretending to be data.

The card’s height is not a constant and the sentinel has to follow it, or “halfway past the block” stops meaning that. Three moves so far: 148 → 122 (one table instead of two), → 110 (row label dropped), phone → 102 (Truck Stop row added). Measured after: condenses at 56px of scroll on a 110px card and 52px on a 102px card — half, to within one scroll step.

Two geometry facts made “one card” real rather than nearly: an absolutely positioned panel resolves left:0 against the padding box, so it sat 1px inside and jogged down both edges — left:-1px;right:-1px fixes it. And the max-content bar measured 365px against a 400px panel, so the bar is now pinned to the card’s width. Measured after: block, bar and panel all at left 425, width 400; page shift on condense still 0px at both widths.

Two footer columns on a phone are no harder to hit than one

The footer went from 515px to 324px — 191px shorter — with every link padded to a 169×44 hit area. The packing worry is testable, so it was tested: aim at the centre of each link’s visible text, offset by every point within a radius, and hit-test what the browser actually returns.

One column and two columns give an identical curve: 0% wrong at ±18px of aim error, 0.1% at ±22, 2.6% at ±26, 5.9% at ±30. Identical because the failure mode is vertical and vertical spacing did not change. Classifying every wrong hit at ±30px across 22,568 samples: 5.9% vertical neighbours, 0.0% horizontal. Not one crossed the gutter, and none can — the smallest sideways error that leaves a target is 29px, and the next column is that plus a 12px gutter away.

The 14px row gap stays. At ±30px, 9.3% of touches land on it and do nothing — which is the gap earning its place. A touch that does nothing beats a touch that navigates somewhere the guest did not ask for; closing the gap would convert those no-ops into wrong destinations.

The Truck Stop page proves the rules are rules, not cases

Built from the same component with nothing added. The Truck Stop leads the table, so diesel and DEF are the first numbers a driver hits; it owns the condensed bar; and Exit 260 is what moves into the panel on condense. That falls out of “the panel carries what is not on screen” once you read the page’s own as the page’s subject rather than always a Location. The callout is gone (a page never advertises itself) and all three Location cards stay — /truck-stop’s self is the callout, not the Exit 260 Location.

Same condense point as every other page: 56px of scroll on the 110px desktop card, 52px on the 102px phone card. Zero page shift, edges aligned, panel inside the viewport — all six frames.

Building it found a real defect in the phone interior page. The panel carried the condensed-only class on the Truck Stop’s label but not its price cells, so at rest the label vanished and three orphan prices stayed in the grid, shifting every row after them. Hide a row by hiding all of its cells, or it is not a row. Renaming that class from ts to oncond is what surfaced it — a class named for a place hides the fact that it means a state.

The header nav now passes the same touch test

It failed before: items 26 / 43 / 48px wide against a 44px guideline. Fixed by spending the bar’s height, which is cheap, and its width, which is not. Nav items and the Rewards pill are 44px-tall targets, nav items 53 and 58px wide, and the phone bar grows 52 → 56px. That 4px is the whole cost, once, on a sticky header.

Same simulation as the footer: 0% wrong at ±30px of aim error, 0.9% at ±34. Better than the footer’s 2.6% at ±26, for a structural reason — the footer’s neighbours are above and below, the header’s are beside, split by a 4px gutter with nothing above or below the bar. Vertical overshoot leaves the bar and hits nothing: 16% of touches at ±30px land on no target, which is the right outcome for a slip.

Width budget, available / needed: 375px → 349/325, 360 → 334/279, 320 → 294/279. No overflow at any of them — 320px phones were previously broken. The pill drops “Get the” below 370px, the same call already made for the condensed bar’s affordance.

Two things worth keeping. The phone nav is flex-wrap:nowrap now: it wrapped silently to a second row before, which is the worst failure mode for a sticky header — double chrome on every page, and nobody notices in review. And the pill’s hit area is the anchor, not the cedar shape, so the button keeps its size while the target gets its 44px.

The phone header has no room for a three-item nav

Measured at 375px — the widest common phone — the 52px bar needs exactly its 349px of usable width for logo + three nav items + the “Get the App” pill. Zero slack, so at 360px or 320px it wraps to a second row, and a two-row sticky header costs that height on every page and every scroll.

Decided: on a phone the Market lockup carries Home — it already links there — and the nav shows Locations · Truck Stop. All three destinations stay reachable. An icon-only Rewards pill also fits (59px to spare) but strips the words off a decision made to keep Rewards prominent; a hamburger hides three destinations behind a tap.

The tap targets that were open here are fixed — see the card above. The bar is 56px now, and the width budget it left is what the pill’s narrow-screen label is spending.

Surplus page height belongs at the foot, not between the rows

Home carried a 65px hole under the headline. The frame had a min-height taller than its content, and CSS grid shared that surplus between the rows rather than leaving it at the bottom. align-content:start on the page grid is the rule; the hole was the symptom. Worth knowing because it looks like a spacing bug in the hero and is not.

A promo with real copy must not carry an aria-label

The promos carried aria-label text from when they were empty dashed boxes. An aria-label overrides an element’s own text, so the moment they gained a real eyebrow and headline a screen reader would have announced the placeholder instead of the offer — on every promo, sitewide. Removed. ADR 0007’s rule stands with one correction: it applies to image-only promos, and the accessible name must be the offer and must not be two things.

The card line is where it is and when it is open — nothing else

One line: street, then hours. The sentence it replaced described what each place sells, which is the amenities list’s job on the detail page — a list view is for choosing between places. Rows go 78 → 61px, the three-row list 439 → 199px against where it started, and the desktop card 108 → 89px, because this is a change to the field rather than a phone-only string.

The line truncates rather than wrapping, so its length is a copy budget. Slack past the longest current string: 74px at 375, 59px at 360, 33px at 320 — about five characters on the narrowest common phone. Hours must stay in the 6am–9pm form; 6:00 AM – 9:00 PM does not fit, and the hours are what get cut because the street comes first.

A container query adds no specificity. The narrow-screen step-down was written as .loccard p and silently lost to the .phone .loccard p rule above it — measured no change at 320px, which is the only reason it was caught. Matching the selector inside the query took the slack from 19px to 33px.

The obvious fix was measured first, and it lost

Three location cards ran 439px on a 375px screen. Three-across is the reflex answer and it is worse: 88px columns, “Fisherman’s” rendering 100px wide inside an 88px box, body copy at 8 lines, and only a 150px saving. Compact rows — motif, name, two clamped lines, chevron — give 78px a row, 249px for three and stay readable — and 61px a row once the description became one line. Building the rejected option for ten minutes is cheaper than arguing about it.

The float landed on the one link the brand rules protect

Measured at full scroll before any gutter, the Back-to-top disc sat directly on “Lummi Commercial Companies” with 0px of clearance, and came within 2px of a footer link on all three desktop pages. That is the only place other Lummi companies appear anywhere on the site. The footer now reserves 78px rather than the button learning to dodge — a dodging button needs a docked state, a threshold and a transition, and still covers something the one time the threshold is wrong. After: 52–60px clear on every page.

A control that arrives after you do is worse than no control

Back-to-top at a 0.5-viewport threshold appears for the final 3% of two of these pages. At 1.0 it reaches only the last 19% of the one page long enough to want it. 0.75 covers 39% of Home and stays silent on the other five — which run 1.38–1.56 screens and genuinely do not need it. The threshold is the feature; the button is the easy part.

An ADR asserted two things that cannot both be built

ADR 0007 said the promo sits above the page title and that it is a full-width region below the two-column top. The title lives inside that two-column top, so on desktop the second sentence makes the first impossible — measured, title at 92px and promo at 205px, with no arrangement that reverses it. On a phone the choice was real and the numbers settle it: title at 388px with the promo above, 190px below. Found while correcting stale pixel counts, which is usually how.

Still placeholder, still blocking

The logo lockup, the three motifs and the promo artwork are dashed boxes on purpose. Coast Salish formline is specific to Lummi Nation and the guardrail is that final art must be authentic, commissioned, or tribe-approved. The copy is first-draft placeholder — copy-editor’s work against the three source sites. And the header still cannot carry the real logo: a navy wordmark on a navy bar (ADR 0006).

Register

The register

The nine concerns the design pulled in. C1–C4 are decided and written into ADR 0005, which is committed. C5, C7 and C9 stay open with recommendations — C7 is a business call, not a design one. C6 and C8 are settled by later ADRs and kept here for the reasoning. None of them causes rework if settled later.

C1 · decided

Dismissing the panel, against the zero-JavaScript goal

Native <details> closes only from its own control. A guest who opens the panel, sees it cover the page, and taps the page behind it expects it to go away. Nothing happens. Escape does nothing either. The skill currently promises “expand, collapse and keyboard access with zero JavaScript” — that promise is what is in tension, not the feature.

ALeave it. The control toggles both ways and that is all. Cost: an overlay a guest cannot dismiss the way every other overlay on the web is dismissed.
BAdd ~10 lines of script for outside-click and Escape. Cost: breaks the zero-JS promise. Still server-rendered; still works with script off, minus the two conveniences.
CSwap <details> for the HTML Popover API — popover="auto" plus a popovertarget button. Cost: a newer platform feature, and pinning the panel to the block needs CSS anchor positioning.

Decided — C, the popoverPlate D above is it, running for real. I tested C in this container’s Chromium 141: outside-click and Escape both close the panel with no script at all, the page does not reflow, and anchor positioning keeps the panel glued to the block through a scroll. It also makes C2 mostly disappear, since a popover renders in the browser’s top layer above every z-index. I could only test Chromium here — this container has no Safari or Firefox. Popover itself is broadly supported; CSS anchor positioning is the risky half and needs a real check on Safari and Firefox before we commit. If anchor positioning is not safe, the bottom-sheet placement sidesteps it entirely (a viewport-pinned panel needs no anchor), otherwise fall back to B.

C2 · decided

“A z-index high enough” needs a written scale

Without one, the next person who needs something on top writes 9999 and the one after that writes 10000. The substantive question is the last line: a mobile nav menu should cover the price panel, not lose to it — one is navigation, the other is information.

AAdd four layer tokens to skill brand-system. Cost: that skill is LOCKED, and stacking order is not brand.
BRecord the scale in the ADR and the display skill; leave brand-system untouched. Cost: none that I can find.
CNo scale. Cost: the bidding war above.

Decided — B, recorded in ADR 0005 brand-system governs colour, type and spacing. Stacking order is structure, not brand, so it does not belong there even if the file were open. Putting it in the ADR resolves the “touches a locked file” problem rather than working around it. Scale: waterline 1, block 40, panel 50, header and nav 60. If C1 lands on the popover, the panel line becomes advisory — the top layer outranks all of it.

C3 · decided

Home’s panel sits in the layout; every other page’s overlays

Home loads expanded. An overlay drawn on arrival would cover the hero before anyone touched it, so on Home the panel takes real space and collapsing it lets the page rise 80px. That is one component with two panel behaviours, selected by whether the block loads open.

AAccept it: loads-open means in flow, loads-shut means overlay. Cost: one branch in the component, and a rule to remember.
BHome loads collapsed like everywhere else. Cost: loses the prominence you asked for on the main page.
CHome gets a separate hero component. Cost: two components to keep in sync — the thing we have been avoiding all session.

Decided — A, accepted The branch is one CSS class and it follows from a rule that states itself: a panel that is already open on arrival cannot overlay anything. B throws away a requirement you gave me; C reintroduces duplication.

C4 · decided

Which placement ships

Plate C is the exploration you asked for and nothing is chosen yet. The measurements: a sticky block holds 134px of every phone screen permanently; docked costs nothing but is gone once a guest scrolls; corner covers the page headline even when shut and wants the top-right slot the locked architecture reserves for Rewards; bottom puts the control in thumb reach and opens upward.

ADocked everywhere. Cost: a driver deep in a page scrolls back up to check a price.
BDocked on desktop, bottom sheet on phones. Cost: two placements to build; reads app-like on phones.
CSticky everywhere. Cost: the permanent 134px, on the screen size that can least afford it.

Decided — corner, on both widthsOverrides my bottom-sheet recommendation. Two consequences follow, both now recorded in ADR 0005.First, corner is the one placement that cannot avoid CSS anchor positioning — a bottom sheet is pinned to the viewport, a corner card has to be told where its corner is. That makes anchor positioning a hard dependency, and only Chromium was testable here. The ten-line script remains the fallback and the component’s shape does not change if we need it. Second, at 375px the card is 353px wide and floats over the page content shut as well as open, so it obscures the page’s own headline persistently rather than only while expanded. The Rewards collision that sat here is resolved in Plate E — see below.

C5

The panel can outgrow the screen

Four places and two grades fit today at 212px. They stop fitting when a guest turns text size up — which the people most likely to squint at a fuel price are most likely to have done — or if a fifth place or third grade ever appears.

ACap the panel at min(70vh, 26rem) with overflow-y:auto. Cost: a scrollbar inside the panel in the rare tall case.
BNothing; four places always fit. Cost: at 200% text the panel runs off the bottom of the phone and the last rows are unreachable.

Recommend — A Two lines of CSS against a failure that is invisible in normal testing and total for the guest who hits it.

C6

The block duplicates the Fuel Prices page

/fuel-prices is a page whose whole content is all locations in one view — reached from the footer and the “All prices” panel rather than the nav, since ADR 0008 took the nav to three items. The block shows the same thing. Nobody has said what happens when they meet — today the spec puts the block on every page, so that page gets prices twice.

AOn /fuel-prices the block is the page — expanded, in flow, no separate strip above it. Cost: reuses the Home in-flow behaviour from C3, so nothing new.
BSuppress the block on that page and hand-build the page. Cost: a second thing rendering prices — the duplication we killed in ADR 0004.
CLeave both. Cost: the same prices twice on one screen.

Recommend — A It falls straight out of C3: a block that loads expanded sits in the layout. The Fuel Prices page is then the same component with a different default, plus whatever copy and hours belong around it. One renderer of prices, sitewide.

C7

A posted price the pump does not honour

The site is static. A price changes in TinaCMS, the rebuild takes a minute or two, and a CDN-cached page can lag further. For most content that is fine. A fuel price is a number a customer drives across town for, and the three sites we are merging are a retail business, not a blog.

AShow the updated stamp in the expanded panel and carry a short “prices subject to change” line. Cost: one row of small type; makes the stamp question C9 load-bearing.
BStamp only, no disclaimer. Cost: the date implies currency without saying prices can move.
CNeither. Cost: a stale price reads as a promise.

Recommend — A This is the one concern on the page that is not a design question. Worth putting to whoever owns pricing at the company rather than deciding in a build session — I am flagging it, not settling it.

C8

A block on every page is a block a screen reader meets on every page

The block sits above the page’s own heading sitewide. Someone navigating by landmark or heading hits fuel prices before the content they came for, on every single navigation.

AWrap it in <aside aria-label="Fuel prices"> so it is one skippable landmark, and keep the control a real button. Cost: none. It is the correct element regardless.
BA generic <div>. Cost: four unlabelled price rows before the page content, every page.
