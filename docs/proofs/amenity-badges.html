Amenity Badge Row

You asked what “as a badge row” means. Fair question — it has been written down three times and never actually drawn. location-content-model says “amenities: string list (drives badges/icons — never hand-place per page)”, and ADR 0009 refers to it twice as if it exists. No proof file renders one. So here it is, in three treatments, with the real amenity list from each Location rather than filler.

The short version: a badge row is the “what’s here” strip on a Location page — one small badge per amenity, icon plus two or three words, wrapping across the page instead of running down it as a bulleted list. It is the row you scan in a second at a fuel stop, not something you read.

One word, not two. An earlier draft of this page said “chip” in places and “badge” in others. They meant the same object — one rounded outline with an icon and a couple of words in it, like ⛽ Fuel — and that object is called a badge from here on. “Chip” is interface jargon for the same shape and has been removed.

Decided 17 Sep 2026: Treatment A, pill badges. The rest of this page is kept as the reasoning behind that choice and as the record of what it costs. Two things follow from it and are now rules rather than options: the row suppresses itself below two badges, so the Minimart cannot ship as a single badge under a heading promising a list (§3); and the amenities field stays free text with a fallback dot for anything the icon set does not cover, so adding “propane” never needs an engineer (§5).

Why A is the recommendation. A list runs down the page and pushes the fuel prices, the map link and the Truck Stop summary below the fold. The badge row runs across and costs one or two lines total. On a fuel stop the amenity list is the thing people scan and the thing they stop reading after three items — a horizontal row suits that, a vertical list argues for reading all of it.

B is the cheaper insurance. No border means no badge that looks broken when there is only one of them — which is the Minimart’s exact problem, §3 below.

Look at the Minimart. One badge, alone, under a heading that promises a list. It does not read as “this place has one thing” — it reads as “the other badges failed to load.” That is not a rendering bug, it is the honest state of the data: the Minimart’s recorded amenity list is the single string “fuel + convenience store”.

This is the whole reason the real amenity list per Location was one of the three questions that can change work already done. The badge row is not a layout that sits on top of the data — it is the data, drawn. Four amenities make a row; one makes a mistake.

Illustrative — these three are guesses, not confirmed.

3 — Suppress the row under two

What’s here

Fuel and a convenience store, open 7am–8pm, next to Silver Reef Casino.

The recommendation is 1 + 2, with 3 as the guard rail. Split the string whatever else happens — “fuel + convenience store” is two facts typed into one field, and the badge row is where that shows. Then the real list is a five-minute question to whoever works there: coffee, ATM, lottery, propane, air, ice, restrooms, a deli case. Every one of those is a reason a driver picks one exit over the next.

Rule 3 stays in the component regardless: fewer than two amenities and the row does not render, the sentence does. A component that cannot draw a bad state badly is worth more than one that always draws.

5. What it costs — the part that needs a decision

A plain list takes any words an editor types. A badge row does not, because every badge wants an icon, and an icon has to be drawn in advance by somebody.

OptionHow amenities behaves in the CMSWhat happens to a new amenity
Free text + fallback dot
chosen		 Staff type anything, as today. The component matches the text against a small icon map. Renders immediately with a neutral dot instead of an icon. Nothing breaks, nothing is blocked, and the icon gets drawn later if the amenity sticks around.
Fixed picklist A dropdown of approved amenities. Every one has an icon by definition. Staff cannot add it. It is an engineering ticket — exactly the kind of thing this build is meant to avoid.
Icon per item in the CMS Each amenity is a row with a label and an icon picker. Works, but hands a design decision to whoever is editing hours that morning.

Free text with a fallback is the only one that keeps the promise the whole build is built on — staff change content without an engineer. The icon map is an optimisation on top of it, not a gate in front of it. A missing icon is a slightly plainer badge; a picklist is a phone call.

  • The icons above are stand-ins. They are utility glyphs — pump, truck, shower — drawn for this proof so the row could be seen. They are not Coast Salish motifs and are not governed by the art rules; the real set is either licensed or drawn once by the designer. TODO: replace with the final icon set.
  • The row is not a link. No badge navigates anywhere. The moment one does, every one of them has to, and the amenity list becomes a nav.
  • Order is the order in the field, not alphabetical. Whoever writes the list puts the reason-to-stop first, and that is the correct instinct to preserve.
  • The Truck Stop’s badges come from its own record, never merged with the store’s (location-content-model). Showers are the truck stop’s amenity, not Exit 260’s.
  • It does not appear on the card or the Locations index. A list view is for choosing between places — street and hours only. The badge row is a detail-page block.
