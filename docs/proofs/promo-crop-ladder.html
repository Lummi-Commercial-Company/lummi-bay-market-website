Proof · promo region · spec sheet §07

The promo crop ladder

Section 07 of the asset spec sheet proposed five aspect ratios — one per slot width on the promo ladder. This page is the evidence they were signed off against, on 16 September 2026: the same master image run through every crop at true pixel size, what happens when a row re-divides and a promo is promoted to a wider slot, what a single fixed ratio would cost instead, and how much copy each slot will carry. Section 07 records what was decided — the five ratios as proposed, a 2400 × 1350 master, a 28-character headline and a 30-character body, four fields on desktop and three on the phone, and an editable button label.

The two photographs are stand-ins, generated by scripts/make-crop-proof-sources.py because there is no store photography yet. They are not proposed artwork. 02 / the alternativeWhy one fixed ratio cannot serve the ladder

The obvious simplification is to pick a single aspect ratio and crop everything to it. It does not survive contact with the two constraints that are already locked: a 150px minimum slot height, and a full-width promo that does not swallow the viewport.

F1No single ratio clears both ends of the ladder

The narrowest slot is 290px. To reach the 150px floor it needs a ratio no wider than 290 ÷ 150 = 1.93 : 1. Apply that same ratio to the 1200px full-width slot:

quarter floor 290 / 150 = 1.93 : 1 ← widest ratio the quarter allows full width at 1.93 : 1 1200 / 1.93 = 622 px ← shortest full-width promo it permits laptop viewport (900px) 622 / 900 = 69% ← of the screen, for one promo

So a single-ratio ladder forces a full-width promo at least 622px tall — over two-thirds of a 900px laptop viewport, for what ADR 0007 positions as a secondary element below the page title. The ratio has to vary down the ladder. That is the finding the proposed ladder rests on.

03 / the masterOne 16:9 file, one 5:1 safe band

Because the ratio varies, a designer needs to know what to actually supply. This falls out of the ladder rather than being a separate decision.

F2Every crop in the ladder is a vertical trim of a single 16:9 master

16:9 (1.78:1) is the tallest ratio in the ladder; 5:1 is the widest. Fit the master to the tallest and no crop ever removes anything sideways — the slot is always equal to or wider than the source, so object-fit: cover only ever trims top and bottom.

supply 2400 × 1350 (16:9, 2× the widest slot) tallest crop 16:9 → uses all 1350 rows (quarter, and every phone slot) widest crop 5:1 → uses the centre 480 (full width) intersection 2400 × 480 ← the safe band: survives every crop horizontal loss none, at any slot width

So the rule for a photographer is two numbers, not five: deliver 2400 × 1350, and keep the subject inside the centre 2400 × 480 strip. Everything above and below that strip is atmosphere that may or may not be shown.

One dependency worth naming: the safe band is set by the widest ratio in the ladder. Sign off a full-width crop wider than 5:1 and the band gets thinner for every photograph already shot; sign off a narrower one and the band grows. Changing that one number re-specifies the whole library.

Nobody is asked before this happens

A row re-divides the moment a promo's date window closes, because twelve divides evenly by 1, 2, 3, 4 and 6 and a hole must never open in the layout (ADR 0018). The art is not re-supplied and no one is notified. B was a perfectly good quarter on Monday.

Which is why the band is a supply rule, not a design preference

It is the only instruction that makes a promo safe against a layout change it cannot see coming. Composition outside the band is free — it just cannot be load-bearing.

04 / the textHow much copy the narrowest slot will take

Eyebrow, headline, body and button are live fields staff type, laid over the image (spec sheet §07). They have to fit inside the slot, and the slot heights come from the ladder. This section is the one that changed after measuring: the draft asserted the four fields do not fit the quarter. They do. The constraint is copy length, and it is much tighter than a field count.

Every figure below is read out of the browser by the page itself, at the type sizes of the approved promo card in promo-system.html — eyebrow 8.5px, headline 17px/1.2, button 9px in a 6/11 pill, 13/14 padding. The body field has no approved size because the approved card has no body field; it is set here at 12.5px/1.45, about the smallest a body field plausibly runs, which makes these the most generous numbers available.

F3All four fields fit every slot. Roughly 115 characters is where the quarter breaks

four fields, one line each, any slot width = 113 px quarter slot height = 163 px ------ headroom at the quarter = 50 px — about two extra lines

Read the quarter row as: a headline of about 45 characters plus a body of about 70, and it is full. One more sentence and the text runs off the bottom of the picture. Nothing in the page stops an editor typing that sentence.

The quarter figure was tightened after section 05 was built. The first sweep ran one word pool and returned 130 characters; run against six samples of realistic promo prose it returns 115, because real sentences wrap worse than an evenly-worded string. The table shows the six-sample worst case, which is the only number safe to put in a CMS.

Which points at a different remedy than the draft had. Not "drop the body field" — the field fits — but put character counters on the promo fields and set them to the narrowest slot the promo can end up in. That is every slot, because a row re-divides when a neighbour expires (ADR 0018) and a promo written for full width can be showing as a quarter by Tuesday. A single limit on every promo, enforced in the CMS, costs the full-width slot some copy it could have carried and costs the quarter nothing it could ever have held.

What was actually signed is a good deal tighter than this ceiling: 28 characters of headline and 30 of body (section 07). At that length nothing wraps in any slot, so the ceiling measured here stops being a constraint and becomes headroom — worth knowing if the caps are ever revisited.

An inconsistency worth settling in the same decision

Spec sheet §07 names four live fields — eyebrow, headline, body, button. The approved promo card in promo-system.html renders three: eyebrow, headline, button. There is no body field in the approved design and therefore no approved size for one. The measurements above assume the four-field version because the spec sheet is what a photographer and an editor are handed. If the answer is three fields, the quarter ceiling rises to about 124 characters of headline alone and the body row above is moot. Section 05 puts the two field sets side by side and measures what the difference is worth.

Settled: four fields on desktop, three on the phone — so the spec sheet is right and the approved card is the file that changes. §07, signed 16 Sep 2026

This is what the quiet zone toggle is for

Whatever the shaded strip covers, the photograph cannot use. At the quarter the three-field card leaves two thirds of the frame clear; the four-field card at the §04 cap leaves almost none. The brief to a photographer is different in the two cases, and the brief is written before anyone knows which slot a promo will end up in.

The spill in B is the point, not a mistake in the example

B was written to the §04 draft cap of 45 characters of headline and 85 of body. It overflows the quarter by 7px, because the headline takes two lines and the body three. The cap was measured again against six samples of realistic promo prose and comes back at 45 / 70; the earlier 45 / 85 came from a single evenly-worded string, which wraps more kindly than English does. The argument for three fields is still not that four overflow — it is what four do to the picture underneath, and to how little an editor may then write.

F4The body field costs the same everywhere and buys almost nothing where it is needed

three-field floor, one line each = 89 px four-field floor, one line each = 113 px ------ cost of the body field, before a word = 24 px — identical at every slot width quarter slot = 163 px three fields, worst case = 110 ch (headline alone) four fields, worst case = 115 ch (45 headline + 70 body) ------ what the fourth field buys = 5 ch

Characters are the wrong unit for reading that, though. The overlay grows in whole lines: above the four-field floor the quarter has 50px of headroom, one more headline line costs about 20px and one more body line about 18px. So the quarter grants two extra lines with four fields and about four with three. The body field does not buy an editor more room; it spends the room on a second typographic register.

Read down the last column: the quarter is the only slot where any of this bites. Everywhere else both field sets hold copy longer than anyone will write, so the body field is free. At the quarter it costs 24px of floor to buy five characters.

A third option the comparison surfaces

Keep the body field and hide it below a slot-width threshold. The table puts that threshold in one place: it renders everywhere except the quarter, and drops out there, where it costs 24px of floor to buy five characters. One field in the CMS, two behaviours in the layout, and editors keep a body field for every promo that has room for one. It needs a rule for what the body says when it is not shown: nothing load-bearing may live there, which is the same discipline the safe band imposes on the picture. It also removes the two-number cap — the CMS counts one budget, not a headline cap and a body cap that interact.

Settled, with the threshold at the device rather than the slot: four fields on desktop, three on the phone. Both caps stay, because both fields survive. The note in section 07 records what that costs at the quarter. §07, signed 16 Sep 2026

The button may not be an editable field either

The approved card in promo-system.html hardcodes the label See details — it is not read from the promo document. So the live count on the approved design is two editable fields, not three: eyebrow and headline. Whichever way the body field goes, the button label needs its own answer, and an editable label is the only version that lets a promo say Order ahead or Directions.

Settled: the label is an editable field, defaulting to See details. §07, signed 16 Sep 2026

The phone is the one place nothing is lost

351 × 197 is 16:9, and the master is 16:9, so the phone shows the whole frame. B reads correctly here and only here — which is exactly how a bad crop gets approved, if it is only ever checked on a phone.

The phone is roomier than the quarter, and that is a trap

351 × 197 holds every sample of realistic copy section 05 threw at it; the desktop quarter stops at about 115 characters. So copy written and checked on a phone can be well past what the narrowest desktop slot will hold — and the row that shows it as a quarter is one expiry away.

07 / signedWhat was decided, and the one thing still open

Signed 16 September 2026. Sections 01–06 are the evidence; this section is the answer. Every figure below is now a number to build to rather than a proposal, and the same values are carried in the designer spec sheet and in ADR 0023 — if they ever disagree, the ADR is the record.

Decided

  1. The five ratios — approved as proposed. 5:1 full width · 3:1 wide half · 3:1 half · 2:1 third · 16:9 quarter. Section 02 shows no single fixed ratio can serve the ladder; this set holds every slot between 163 and 265px tall. signed
  2. The master file — approved as proposed. Supply 2400 × 1350, one file per promo, with the subject inside the centre 2400 × 480 strip. 16:9 is the tallest ratio on the ladder and 5:1 the widest, so a 16:9 master is never cropped horizontally and the 5:1 band is the only region guaranteed to survive every slot. This is the instruction that goes to a photographer. signed
  3. The text budget — 28-character headline, 30-character body. Signed well inside the measured ceiling: section 04 put the quarter's limit at about 115 characters, and the signed pair totals 58. F5 below is what that buys — every card sits at its floor, one line per field, in every slot on the ladder, and survives a worst case of 28 wide capitals with 11px to spare at the quarter. Both counters belong in the CMS, set to the same numbers for every promo regardless of the slot it was written for, because a row re-divides when a neighbour expires (ADR 0018). signed
  4. The field set — four fields on desktop, three on the phone. Eyebrow, headline, body and button on desktop; the body field drops out at phone width. The contradiction section 05 opened is settled in favour of the spec sheet: the body field exists, and the approved card in promo-system.html is the file that has to change. One field in the CMS, two behaviours in the layout. signed
  5. The button label is an editable field, defaulting to See details. The approved card hardcodes it, which made the live count on that design two editable fields rather than three. A promo can now say Order ahead, Directions or See what's baking. signed

Still open

One wrinkle in "four on desktop, three on the phone"

The phone slot is 351px wide and the desktop quarter is 290px — so the rule as signed shows the body field in the narrower of the two and hides it in the wider one. That is defensible as a reading rule rather than a fit rule: a phone card is read at arm's length, one at a time, and three fields is the tighter, cleaner unit there. But if the intent was ever "drop it where it is cramped", the threshold wants to be slot width, not device — body at the third and wider, none at the quarter or on the phone. Both fit; it is a one-line change in the component, and it is the only part of this decision that is cheap to revisit later.

The caps are now a style rule, not a safety rule

28 and 30 are less than half the measured ceiling, and F5 shows nothing can overflow any slot even in the worst case. So the counters in the CMS are no longer protecting the layout — they are keeping the copy punchy. That is a real change in what they are for: if an editor ever asks for more room, the answer is an editorial one, and the geometry will carry roughly twice what has been signed.

What is locked and was never up for discussion here

The 12-column ladder and the 150px minimum slot height (ADR 0007), rows re-dividing on expiry (ADR 0018), no copy baked into artwork, no tribal motifs in the promo region, and the filename rule — never ad, advert, banner or sponsored, which ad blockers match on.

What changing the ratios would now cost

They were cheap to change before sign-off and are expensive after a shoot: every delivered frame gets re-cropped by hand, and re-cropping is where centred subjects drift and heads get clipped. The safe band in particular is set by the widest ratio — moving it invalidates the brief every photograph was shot to.
