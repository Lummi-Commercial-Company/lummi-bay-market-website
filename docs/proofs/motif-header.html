This crosses a recorded rule

Skill pnw-tribal-art lists the sticky header under Never — “the locked logo owns it.” That objection is about the lockup's clear space, so the band is inset to clear it entirely and stops before the Rewards pill, both measured at runtime.

It still needs a decision recorded the way ADR 0020 recorded the sasquatch, or the next person reading the skill will find a rule the site visibly breaks.

The art is a placeholder, and looks it on purpose

These are flat silhouettes, not formline. The guardrail exists because imitations of Coast Salish formline can be inaccurate or appropriative — so a placeholder that obviously is not the real thing is the honest option, and it cannot be mistaken for finished art.

Authentic, commissioned or tribe-approved art replaces them before launch.

The phone carries it too

The gap between the lockup's clear space and the pill is narrow, so the band uses mask-repeat: space: only whole motifs are laid down and the leftover is distributed between them. Nothing is ever cut in half, however narrow the space gets.

A tile only repeats if it fits. The mixed strip is four motifs in one tile, far wider than the phone's gap, so no whole tile ever fits and it degrades to one clipped tile — which is what made the strip look cut in half. The phone tiles a single motif instead, which fits at any width the header can produce.

Nothing sits on the band there — the nav collapses to the menu button and the band stops before the pill — so no contrast is spent at any opacity, and the scale slider is free. space tiles both axes, so turning scale down past roughly half the header height gives a second row and the band reads as a shoal rather than a line. Both look deliberate; pick by eye.

Masked, not painted

The band is a mask-image over a token background: shape from the SVG, colour from brand-system. A motif cannot introduce an off-brand colour, and re-tinting the whole band is one token.

Where masks are unsupported the decoration is hidden. Without that branch every motif renders as a solid rectangle across the header — which has happened in this build before.

Behind the nav, the ceiling is almost nothing

The active item sits on a 16% bone highlight already, which spends most of the headroom before any motif exists: it starts at 5.06:1 against a 4.5 floor. A band behind it caps at 6% in bone, 5% in white, 11% in teal — opacities at which the art is barely visible, so you pay the whole contrast budget for something nobody sees.

That is why Clear of the nav is the default. No text sits on the band, nothing is spent, and the opacity becomes a taste decision rather than a compliance one.

Navy deep is not offered

A darker ink would raise contrast rather than eat it, which would solve the problem outright. The obvious candidate is --lb-navy-deep — but skill pnw-tribal-art reserves it for fuel prices and emphasis and states it is not available for decoration, so it is not in the list.

If ownership wants to release it for this, the header band becomes much easier.

What the numbers watch

A lighter band behind the nav raises the background luminance and eats the contrast of the bone text on top. The binding case is Home: it already sits on a 16% bone highlight, so it starts lighter than its neighbours before any motif is added.

The pill's bone ring is the 1.4.11 boundary and needs 3:1, not 4.5.
